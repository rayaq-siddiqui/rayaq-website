Benchmarks against the frameworks

tools/bench_frameworks.py times all 19 zoo models in PyTorch, JAX and TensorFlow, eagerly and compiled, and in rml from each framework's export, after checking that every result agrees. This page walks through that method, then reads the published results: what machine, which versions, where rml wins, where it trails, and why.

In short: each framework runs in its own process, confined with sched_setaffinity to as many CPUs as the thread count, and every timed result first passes a correctness gate against the framework's eager output. The latest run, on a shared 4-vCPU Xeon container with torch 2.14.1, jax 0.12.0 and tensorflow 2.21.0, puts rml ahead of PyTorch, JAX and TensorFlow on average at every batch and thread count, and ahead of the best framework and mode in 80 of 114 cells. Small MLP-shaped models are its strongest; the LSTM, the U-Net, the SSM, the CNN, the RNN and the GCN are where it still trails the best framework. Four threads is slower than one in 38 of 171 rml cells, and the StableHLO route runs within 4–13% of the direct exporters.

The models are defined on {{ link('exporters', 'Exporters and the model zoo') }}, the runner that times rml is rml-graph-run on {{ link('executor', 'Executing a graph', '#graph-run') }}, and the optimizations each gap prompted are on {{ link('lowering', 'Lowering and fast CPU') }}. How CPU and GPU frameworks schedule work in general is on {{ ml('gpu-execution', 'GPU execution') }}.

The method

The script's docstring states the whole method (bench_frameworks.py L1–22). For each framework, model (at its bench size) and batch size it exports the model to models/graphs/<framework>_<model>_b<batch> with a golden file of the framework's outputs, then times three things at each thread count: the framework eagerly, the framework compiled, and rml running the exported graph.

Processes and threads

Each framework runs in a subprocess, so its thread pool is set before the framework starts (L252–265). The driver sets OMP_NUM_THREADS and MKL_NUM_THREADS, and for JAX an XLA_FLAGS with intra_op_parallelism_threads; inside the worker, PyTorch gets set_num_threads and one inter-op thread, TensorFlow one inter-op thread and N intra-op threads (L84–94). None of that is trusted alone: pinned(threads) confines each timed child, and each rml-graph-run, to the first N CPUs with os.sched_setaffinity, because recent JAX ignores the XLA thread flag and runs its matmuls on every core (L242–249). The report's findings open by restating that every timed process ran confined this way (frameworks.md L343). The thread counts default to 1 and every core (L403–404).

The correctness gate

Nothing is timed until it is right. In the framework worker the eager output is the reference, and the compiled output must satisfy |got − want| ≤ 10−4 + 10−3·|want| at every element, or the sweep stops (L53–60, L226–228). The golden file rml checks against holds the framework's own outputs, written at export (L141–168). rml-graph-run --check reports its largest absolute error, which must be at most 10−4 + 10−3 times the largest |want| of the first output, a bound over the whole tensor rather than per element (L277–280).

Timing

A framework cell runs its callable five times untimed, then times runs until it has at least --min-runs (10) and has spent --budget (1 s), up to --max-runs (200), waiting on the result each time so asynchronous dispatch is counted (L63–72). rml runs exactly --rml-runs (50) after the same warmup, timed with steady_clock inside the binary, so process start and graph load are outside the number (graph_run.cc L87–100). Both sides report p10, median and p90 by nearest rank: the value at index ⌈q·n⌉ − 1 of the sorted times (L75–78, graph_run.cc L68–85). The inputs are the same for every route: example_inputs(model, "bench", batch, seed=1) (L219).

The results

The committed report (frameworks.md L1–7) was produced on an Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz, 4 logical CPUs, Linux x86_64, with torch 2.14.1+cu130, jax 0.12.0 and tensorflow 2.21.0, rml built with --config=opt, at 1 and 4 threads. Every value is f32 on CPU. Each model has a table of nine columns per batch and thread count: both modes of each framework and rml from that framework's export, as median (p10–p90) milliseconds; after each rml time, the faster of that framework's two modes divided by rml's time, so above 1× rml is faster (L218). The framework cells come from the earlier full sweep; every rml cell was re-timed with --redo-rml on the current runtime and exporters (L343–347).

The summary is a geometric mean over the 19 models of the faster framework mode's time divided by rml's, with the number of models rml wins. In parentheses, the previous run, before constant-weight packing, the wider fusion groups, GEMM column balancing, the blocked strided copy and the conv bias fold; the last column takes the best of all three frameworks (L349–361):

The two corners still tell different stories. At batch 1 on one thread rml beats PyTorch on all 19 models and TensorFlow on 16. At batch 64 on four threads, where large GEMMs and convolutions dominate, it beats PyTorch on 8 of 19, against 17 for both JAX and TensorFlow (L356–361).

Where rml wins

  • Small and medium MLP-shaped models, as a geometric mean over batches and threads against the best framework: two_tower 2.6×, diffusion_mlp 2.4×, mlp 1.9×, autoencoder and vae 1.7×, gan_generator 1.6× (L367–370). These are a handful of matmuls and elementwise activations, the shapes the few-row GEMM path and fused programs on {{ link('lowering', 'Lowering and fast CPU') }} were built for.
  • Attention models: encoder, clip, vit, tiny_gpt and transformer_block at 1.2–1.4×, with their batch-64 matmuls running at 360–410 GFLOP/s on four threads (L369–370).
  • Against TensorFlow by large factors at small batch, in both its modes; diffusion_mlp at batch 1 on one thread is 0.074 ms in rml from TensorFlow's export against 1.26 ms TF eager and 0.611 ms TF XLA (L156).
  • Graphs that arrive decomposed. layernorm_gelu (1.5× overall) at batch 64 on four threads runs in 23.7 ms from JAX's export against 56.6 ms for jax.jit (2.4×), and 24.2 ms from TensorFlow's against 44.2 ms for TF XLA (1.8×) (L51). JAX and TensorFlow hand this model over with layer norm and GELU written out, and rml's export passes fuse both back into single ops before rml runs it.

Reading it: rml from PyTorch's export now scales from 40.5 to 12.8 ms with four threads (the previous report had 59.5 and 41.9), which is ahead of jax.jit and level with TF XLA but still behind PyTorch's fused ATen LSTM at 8.81 ms. The change came from constant weights packed once at compile time, each step's gates and cell update fused into one program reading the gate slices in place, and GEMM column chunks balanced across threads ({{ link('lowering', 'Lowering and fast CPU', '#lstm') }}). rml also tried folding the recurrence's bias and input-projection adds into the step GEMM and dropped it: the per-step GEMM already runs at 245–290 GFLOP/s on four threads, about the machine's peak, so the remaining gap is the GEMM itself (kernels.md L258–273).

Four threads slower than one

Four threads is slower than one in 38 of rml's 171 cells (19 models × 3 batches × 3 exports), down from 51, almost all at batch 1 with sub-millisecond times (L374–375): mlp from PyTorch at batch 1 is 0.0518 ms on one thread and 0.115 on four, cnn goes from 0.14 to 0.176 and lstm from 1.39 to 1.69 (L13–14, L24–25, L101–102). Waking a pool costs more than such work saves; the grains that keep small ops on the calling thread are on {{ link('lowering', 'Lowering and fast CPU', '#threads') }}.

Direct against StableHLO

The report also times rml on the graph each framework's StableHLO lowered to, against the direct export of the same model (L220–339). The factor is direct / StableHLO, so above 1× the StableHLO graph runs faster. The geometric means are 0.95 for PyTorch, 0.87 for JAX and 0.96 for TensorFlow: the StableHLO route is within 4–13% of the direct one (L376–377). Single cells swing both ways; mlp at batch 1 on one thread is 0.0518 ms direct and 0.0709 ms through StableHLO from PyTorch, but 0.0698 direct and 0.0562 through StableHLO from TensorFlow (L226). Both routes are described on {{ link('exporters', 'Exporters and the model zoo') }}.

Reading the numbers

  • A shared machine. The container is a shared 4-vCPU cloud VM where single cells vary by up to about 30% between runs, so rml's notes say to compare the means (L345–346).
  • Outlier cells. A few framework cells are far off their neighbours: PyTorch on vit at batch 1 on four threads takes 344 ms eager against 5.51 ms on one thread, giving rml about 200× (L57–58), and PyTorch on unet at batch 1 on four threads 151 ms (L168). The report does not explain them; the geometric means are less sensitive to them than any single cell.
  • Mixed sweeps. The framework cells come from the earlier full sweep; every rml cell is from the current runtime and exporters (L344–345), so the framework columns are unchanged from the previous report.
  • Not the target hardware. Apple Silicon numbers, with NEON and Accelerate, have not been produced (L347).
